### 🧍 **General Profile**
- **Age:** Early 30s by human reckoning
- **Ancestry:** Human (coastal Isterian lineage, unverified mixed descent)
- **Appearance:** Lean-built and travel-worn, dressed in layered expedition garb reinforced with stitched warding threads from the Enclave. Her clothing is practical rather than ceremonial, marked with faded Enclave sigils that have been partially scrubbed or rewritten over time. She carries minimal equipment—everything she uses is either replaceable or already accounted for in memory logs.
- **Aura:** Uneasy stillness. Not threatening, but perceptive in a way that makes others feel partially observed even when she is not looking at them directly.
- **Mannerisms:** Constantly scanning environments without obvious focus. Often pauses mid-step as if listening to structural silence rather than sound. Speaks less than necessary, and when she does, it feels like she is summarizing something already concluded elsewhere.
---
### 📖 **Origins**
- Maev Aimes began as a **field-accredited Enclave scout** assigned to validate relic sites before formal expeditions were approved.
- Unlike scholars who interpret divine history through texts, Maev was trained to interpret it through **environmental response**:
    - Stone that resists mapping
    - Corridors that shift after observation
    - Chambers that react to intent rather than motion
- Early in her career, she gained recognition for mapping “impossible” ruins that conventional surveys failed to stabilize.
- Her work introduced a new Enclave sub-discipline: **situational theology mapping**—the study of how environments behave when exposed to relic proximity.

---
### 🕯️ **The Wyrmbane Assignments**
- Maev was among the first scouts assigned to preliminary incursions into **Wyrmbane’s Hollow**.
- Her initial reports described the Hollow as:
    - Non-static geography
    - Memory-reactive architecture
    - Acoustic absence that behaves like resistance rather than silence
- These findings were initially dismissed within the Enclave as miscalibration or psychological fatigue.
Maev, however, continued returning with increasingly consistent anomalies.

---
### 🧭 **Field Philosophy**
- Maev does not treat ruins as dead spaces.
- She treats them as **systems that refuse consensus**.
- Her guiding belief:
    - If multiple observers record different truths in the same place, then the location is not wrong—**interpretation is unstable**.
- This puts her in a subtle ideological tension with Norae Delacroix, who seeks reconstruction, while Maev increasingly documents **non-reconcilable reality states** without forcing alignment.

---
### 🔗 **Relationship to Pyxis**
- Maev and Pyxis operate in adjacent Enclave functions:
    - Pyxis = relic activation and doctrinal convergence
    - Maev = pre-contact verification and spatial integrity assessment
- Maev views Pyxis as inexperienced but structurally important—someone who still believes the Enclave’s interpretations are stable enough to rely on.
- She does not openly correct Pyxis unless necessary, instead allowing experience to “invalidate certainty naturally.”
- Privately, Maev worries Pyxis is being positioned too close to convergence-class relics without sufficient field distortion training.

---
### 🕸️ **Methods**
- **Pre-Entry Mapping:** Records environmental “baseline behavior” before relic interaction begins.
- **Silent Marking:** Uses non-magical markers that only become visible under specific structural conditions.
- **Echo Logging:** Records not just what is seen, but what the environment “refuses to repeat.”
- **Controlled Withdrawal:** Prioritizes exit over confirmation when spatial integrity begins to degrade.
- **Non-Intervention Doctrine:** Avoids influencing relic responses unless survival depends on it.

---
### ⚖️ **Philosophy**

> _“A place that changes when you understand it is not hiding truth. It is resisting ownership.”_

Maev Aimes does not seek divine confirmation.  
She seeks **consistency in instability**.
Unlike Norae, she does not believe every anomaly must resolve into meaning.  
And unlike Pyxis, she does not assume every relic is waiting to be understood.
She believes Wyrmbane’s Hollow is not a puzzle to be solved—
But a place that refuses to agree it is one.
