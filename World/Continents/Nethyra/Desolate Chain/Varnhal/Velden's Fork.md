## Layout
---
Set where three old roads split like prongs around a shallow, stony creek, Velden’s Fork is less a destination and more a place people pass through—though many end up staying longer than intended. Weathered signposts point north to the fog-choked **Mistbarrows**, south toward the distant banners of **Highthrone Bastion**, and east along a narrowing road that winds to the looming silhouette of **Vireholt Keep**.

The village itself huddles close to the crossroads, as if reluctant to stretch too far into the surrounding wilds. The buildings are sturdy but worn, their foundations older than anyone can quite account for. Locals say the roads existed before the village—and that something about the Fork makes it hard to leave once you’ve chosen a direction.

---

**The Crooked Tankard (Tavern)**

Leaning slightly toward the northern road, **The Crooked Tankard** feels like it’s bracing itself against whatever might come out of the Mistbarrows. Inside, the fire is always lit, even in warmer months. The tavern owner, Mary Stout, keeps a ledger not just of tabs—but of names, routes, and odd occurrences. She claims it’s for “memory,” though she’s been known to warn certain travelers not to take certain roads.

The regulars speak in low tones about shapes seen in the northern fog and lights that move where no lantern should.

---

**Thistle & Vial (Potion Shop)**

On the eastern edge of the square sits **Thistle & Vial**, its crooked frame reinforced with mismatched beams. Fenrick, the resident herbalist, sources strange reagents from all three directions—ashen roots from the south, ironbark resin from the east, and pale, whisper-thin fungi said to grow only in the Mistbarrows.

His potions are reliable, but some come with peculiar side effects: dreams of places you’ve never been, or fleeting glimpses of figures standing just out of sight.

He drives the hardest bargain claiming his potions are to storng for the party.

---

**The Fork Square**

At the center lies a triangular square formed by the three converging roads. A weathered stone marker stands at its heart, carved with faded symbols no one has fully deciphered. Some claim it marks an agreement, others a warning. A dry fountain—long since cracked—circles the stone, now filled with candles, coins, and the occasional offering left by uneasy travelers.

The square is rarely empty. Messengers from Highthrone pass through with urgency, traders from the east linger under heavy cloaks, and few who come down from Mistbarrows stay past dusk.
