## 1. The Players' Arrest

After the players present Queen Caelith Veyron with evidence of her dealings with the Dollmaker and the creation of half-mechanical creatures, she orders their arrest. They are taken beneath Highthrone Palace and placed within the Intelligence Quiet Rooms, isolated from the outside world and surrounded by wards designed to prevent their conversation from being overheard.  
Caelith does not immediately appear. Instead, Natali is sent to question them. Her questions are calm and deliberate. She wants to know what they have discovered, how much they understand about the Dollmaker, whether they have connected Caelith to his work, and, most importantly, whether they intend to expose the Queen. Rella is not interested in threatening them. She is interested in understanding exactly how much they know.  
_"You have been remarkably thorough,"_ Natali might say, slowly looking over the evidence they have gathered. _"Which makes me wonder. Did you find all of this yourselves, or has someone been helping you?"_  
Once Natali has learned enough, Caelith enters the room herself. She dismisses Natali and takes a seat opposite the players. For several moments, she simply studies them.  
_"You have been asking questions that very few people are brave enough to ask."_  
_"You have also been looking in places where you were never meant to look."_  
She pauses.  
_"So perhaps you deserve to know something."_

## 2. A Sliver of Honesty
Caelith does not deny what the players have uncovered. Instead, she gives them something far more dangerous: a piece of the truth.  
She tells them that she knows who they are.  
_"You are not the first."_  
_"You will not be the last."_  
_"And yes... I know about the Quell Trials."_  
Caelith explains that the players are participants in something far older and larger than they realize. The Quell Trials have existed for years, producing groups of adventurers who are gradually pushed toward one final challenge: Krallax.  
The players have likely been told that if they are strong enough, clever enough, or determined enough, they may eventually be capable of slaying him.  
Caelith looks almost ashamed when she confirms it.  
_"That is what you were supposed to believe."_  
_"That there is a path through this. That somewhere beneath all of this horror is a weakness. That if you are simply good enough... you might be the ones who succeed."_  
She looks away.  
_"I have told that story many times."_  
_"Too many."_  
For years, Caelith has been ordered to give the participants of the Quell Trials hope. Every group that reaches a certain point is given the same promise: Krallax can be defeated. The Dollmaker knows how. The right people can find the right weakness.  
But Caelith has watched those people fail.  
Again and again.  
_"I used to believe it,"_ she admits. _"That is the worst part."_  
_"But after enough years, you begin to recognize a pattern."_  
_"They arrive believing they are the ones who will succeed. They learn about Krallax. They fight. They lose."_  
She looks directly at the players.  
_"And then another group takes their place."_  
The hope was never real.  
At least, not in the way the players were led to believe.

## 3. Thorne Quell

Caelith does not speak Thorne Quell's name easily.  
When she finally does, her voice becomes quieter.  
_"This was not my idea."_  
_"The Trials. The promises. The Dollmaker. None of it."_  
She explains that Thorne Quell is the one who commands the Quell Trials and that Caelith has been one of his instruments for years. Her assignment has always been simple: keep the Trials alive.  
Give the participants hope.  
Let them believe that they might be different.  
Let them believe that Krallax can be defeated.  
_"Thorne does not need them to win,"_ Caelith says. _"He needs them to keep trying."_  
_"Every failure creates another story. Every survivor becomes another reason for the next group to believe."_  
She pauses before adding:  
_"And I was instructed to make sure they never stopped believing."_  
Caelith has obeyed for years because she had little choice. Thorne holds power over her that the players may not yet understand.  
But there is another person trapped beneath that same chain.  
The Dollmaker.

## 4. The Dollmaker

Caelith tells the players that the Dollmaker is not the monster they believe him to be.  
_"You will hate him when you see what he has made."_  
_"You should."_  
_"But do not mistake the things he has done for the man he once was."_  
The Dollmaker was once a brilliant and compassionate creator. He was fascinated by magic, machinery, and the ways in which the two could work together. He wanted to create things that could protect people. He wanted to understand the limits of life and magic.  
Then Caelith found him.  
Or rather, Thorne found him through Caelith.  
The Dollmaker was forced into service. Years of imprisonment, experimentation, threats, and psychological manipulation slowly destroyed his understanding of what he was doing. Caelith was forced to make him work, and Thorne was the one forcing Caelith.  
Eventually, something inside the Dollmaker broke.  
He began to believe his own work was necessary.  
He began convincing himself that every terrible experiment served some greater purpose.  
_"That is what makes him so dangerous now,"_ Caelith says.  
_"He no longer needs me to tell him that what he is doing is right."_  
_"He tells himself."_

## 5. Mudstitch's Shield

When the players mention Mudstitch, the Hillwarden family, or the shield they are searching for, Caelith's expression changes.  
She knows it.  
_"The shield..."_  
For the first time, she seems genuinely surprised.  
The Hillwarden shield was one of the most important objects in the Dollmaker's early research. Its magic was ancient and unusually resilient. It did not simply block attacks. It protected the person carrying it on a deeper level, anchoring their body and spirit against forces that should have destroyed them.  
The Dollmaker became fascinated with it.  
He wondered whether the same protective magic could be applied to something that was not entirely alive.  
And eventually, Caelith ordered him to find out.  
The shield became part of the research that would eventually create Krallax.  
_"Your family carried that shield for generations,"_ Caelith tells Mudstitch. _"And the Dollmaker saw it as something else."_  
_"A solution."_  
The shield was eventually bound into Krallax's construction. Its magic acted as a stabilizing force, allowing the immense magical and mechanical energies within him to coexist.  
It did not make Krallax.  
But without it, Krallax may never have survived.  
Caelith looks at Mudstitch.  
_"Your family heirloom is inside the creature you have been preparing to kill."_

## 6. Krallax

Krallax was not the Dollmaker's first creation.  
He was his first nightmare.  
The Dollmaker's earlier experiments had been attempts to understand preservation. Could a mind survive inside a mechanical body? Could magic protect a living consciousness while the body around it changed? Could something be rebuilt without destroying the person it once was?  
The shield gave him part of the answer.  
Krallax became the culmination of all of it.  
The shield provided stability. The Dollmaker's research provided the machinery. Years of forced experimentation provided the knowledge.  
And Thorne provided the purpose.  
_"They wanted something that could not be broken,"_ Caelith says.  
_"So he made it."_  
_"They wanted something that could survive every champion they sent against it."_  
_"So he made that too."_  
She stops.  
_"And eventually, they stopped asking him to make a weapon."_  
_"They asked him to make a god."_  
Caelith does not tell the players whether Krallax truly became such a thing.  
She simply tells them that nobody has ever defeated him.

## 7. The Lie of Hope

Caelith admits that she has been ordered to give every promising group the same hope.  
The hope that the Dollmaker knows how to defeat Krallax.  
The hope that there is a hidden weakness.  
The hope that they might be the ones who succeed.  
_"Perhaps there is a weakness,"_ she says.  
_"Perhaps there isn't."_  
_"I honestly don't know anymore."_  
For the first time, Caelith seems tired rather than threatening.  
_"That is the truth I can give you."_  
_"I have lied to people for years. I told them they could win because I was told to tell them."_  
_"And every time they believed me, I watched them walk toward him."_  
She looks at the players.  
_"I will not tell you that you can defeat Krallax."_  
_"I will not tell you that you cannot."_  
_"But if you want to know what he is... speak to the man who made him."_  
The Dollmaker is still imprisoned beneath Highthrone Palace.  
Caelith offers to let the players meet him.  
Not because she trusts them.  
Not because she suddenly wants to help them.  
But because she wants to know what they will do with the truth.

## 8. The Dollmaker's Room

If the players eventually meet the Dollmaker, they do not find a terrifying mastermind surrounded by his creations.  
They find a broken man.  
The room is quiet when they enter. Mechanical components hang from the ceiling, strange instruments cover tables, and half-finished creations sit beneath sheets of cloth. The air smells of oil, metal, and old magic.  
But the Dollmaker is not working.  
He stands in the center of the room, staring at something suspended from the ceiling.  
At first, the players may not understand what they are looking at.  
Then they recognize a person.  
Or what remains of one.  
The body has been mutilated by experimentation. Metal has been inserted into flesh, arcane threads run through the body, and various parts have been removed and replaced.  
The Dollmaker is crying.  
His hands are wrapped around the hand of the body as though he is comforting someone who is still alive.  
This is Vexen Dourstep.  
An old friend.  
Someone who knew the Dollmaker before Caelith ever found him.  
Someone who remembered who he was.  
The Dollmaker whispers to him.  
_"I'm sorry, Vexen."_  
_"I know. I know."_  
_"I'm almost finished."_  
He wipes his eyes.  
_"You'll understand when it's done."_  
When the players finally interrupt him, he turns.  
For a moment, there is genuine fear in his expression.  
Then confusion.  
Then recognition.  
_"You're here..."_  
He looks past them, almost expecting someone else to be standing there.  
_"Did she send you?"_  
If the players ask who, he does not answer immediately.  
_"The Queen?"_  
A pause.  
_"No..."_  
_"She wouldn't send you."_  
His eyes drift back toward Vexen.  
_"Not unless she wants something."_

## 9. The Hillwarden Name

If the players mention Mudstitch or the Hillwarden family, the Dollmaker suddenly becomes still.  
_"Hillwarden..."_  
He repeats the name quietly.  
Then he approaches them.  
_"I knew one of them."_  
He looks toward Vexen.  
_"A long time ago."_  
He remembers the shield.  
He remembers its owner.  
He remembers what he wanted to learn from it.  
_"Your grandfather carried something very old."_  
_"A shield."_  
_"It protected him from things that should have killed him."_  
The Dollmaker smiles faintly, almost sadly.  
_"I thought that meant it could protect anything."_  
Then his smile disappears.  
_"I was wrong."_  
He tells them about the shield's connection to his experiments and eventually admits what happened to it.  
_"They wanted me to make something stronger."_  
_"Something that could survive."_  
_"So I used the shield."_  
His hands begin shaking.  
_"I thought I was helping."_  
He looks at his own hands.  
_"I thought if I could make it work, then maybe they would stop."_  
The players may ask who "they" are.  
The Dollmaker looks toward the ceiling.  
_"Everyone."_  
_"The Queen. The man above her. Everyone who kept telling me I was doing something important."_  
_"They kept saying I was saving lives."_  
He looks back at Vexen.  
_"I believed them."_

## 10. Vexen Dourstep

The players may ask about the body hanging in the room.  
The Dollmaker becomes defensive immediately.  
_"Don't."_  
_"Please."_  
He steps between them and Vexen.  
_"He was my friend."_  
_"He was the only one who remembered me before all of this."_  
The Dollmaker explains that Vexen tried to stop the experiments. He tried to convince the Dollmaker that Caelith was manipulating him and that the things they were creating were becoming monsters.  
But the Dollmaker refused to listen.  
Or perhaps he was too afraid to listen.  
Eventually, Vexen became one of the experiments himself.  
_"He said I was becoming something I didn't recognize."_  
The Dollmaker's voice cracks.  
_"He was right."_  
He looks at Vexen.  
_"I did this to him."_  
Then, almost immediately, he begins trying to justify it.  
_"But I can fix him."_  
_"I just need more time."_  
_"I can fix all of it."_  
That is the tragedy of the Dollmaker.  
He knows what he has done.  
But he has been manipulated for so long that he cannot accept what it means.  
He still believes there is a way to make everything right.

## 11. The Truth About Krallax

Eventually, the Dollmaker tells the players what Krallax really is.  
Krallax was never meant to be a weapon.  
Not at first.  
He was meant to be proof.  
Proof that the Dollmaker could create something that would not break.  
Proof that a living mind could survive impossible transformation.  
Proof that magic and machinery could become something greater than either alone.  
But the experiments continued.  
The pressure continued.  
And eventually, Krallax became something else entirely.  
The Dollmaker's greatest creation.  
His greatest failure.  
His greatest nightmare.  
_"They kept asking me to make him stronger."_  
_"So I did."_  
_"They kept asking me to make him harder to kill."_  
_"So I did."_  
_"Then they asked me to make something that could defeat anything they put in front of him."_  
He closes his eyes.  
_"And I did that too."_  
The Dollmaker tells them that the shield remains bound within Krallax, acting as one of the anchors holding his immense power together.  
_"If you want to understand him..."_  
He looks directly at Mudstitch.  
_"You need to understand the shield."_  
Then he looks toward the players.  
_"And if you want to kill him..."_  
A long silence.  
_"You need to understand what we did to him."_

## 12. What Caelith Never Told Them

The players were never supposed to hear any of this.  
Caelith's role in the Quell Trials was to give them enough truth to keep them moving, but never enough to understand the system around them.  
She was supposed to tell them that Krallax could be defeated.  
She was supposed to point them toward the Dollmaker.  
She was supposed to make them believe that they were different from everyone who came before them.  
That they might be the ones who finally succeed.  
But something has changed.  
Caelith has spent years giving people false hope, and for the first time she has decided to give someone a piece of the truth instead.  
Whether that is because she genuinely wants the players to succeed, because she wants Krallax destroyed, or because she simply cannot bear to watch another group walk blindly toward the same fate is unclear.  
The players leave the encounter with something no previous participants were given.  
Not hope.  
The truth.  
**Krallax was built to be the impossible enemy.**  
**The Dollmaker was forced to build him.**  
**Caelith was forced to force the Dollmaker.**  
**And above them all stands Thorne Quell, waiting to see whether the next group of heroes will finally prove him wrong.**
