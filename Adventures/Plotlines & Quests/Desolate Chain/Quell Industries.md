## Main idea
The people of the Desolate Chain believe the Trials were created out of necessity. According to the stories spread throughout the islands, the Chain is a doomed and hostile place that can only survive through strength. [[Krallax]], the great terror lurking beyond civilization, is presented as the ultimate enemy — a monster so catastrophic that only the strongest adventurers could ever hope to defeat it. The Trials exist to forge those heroes. Or so the people are told.

The truth is far crueler. [[Thorne Quell]] never created the Trials to save anyone. He created them to satisfy his own obsession with power, worship, and control. In an age where the old gods have long since been forgotten, Thorne desired to become something greater than mortal. Not a king, not a ruler, but a god. The arena became his temple. The participants became sacrifices offered willingly to his spectacle. That is why his voice echoes throughout the arena.

The magically projected sermons and announcements are not meant merely to guide contestants. They are performances. Thorne speaks from above like a divine being passing judgment upon those beneath him. Entire generations have grown up hearing his unseen voice thunder across the Trial grounds, believing something all-seeing watches over them. Fear, hope, desperation — every emotion strengthens the illusion he has built around himself.

The Trials were never designed to be won. [[Krallax]] was never meant to die. Instead, the entire system exists to maintain control over the population of the Desolate Chain. The strong are glorified before eventually dying. The weak are culled. Society remains desperate enough to obey, but hopeful enough to continue feeding the machine. Hidden behind the spectacle lies an elaborate network of manipulation sustained by Quell Industries, [[High Regent Caelith Veyron]], [[Characters/NPC/Backstory NPCs/The Dollmaker|The Dollmaker]], and countless others trapped within Thorne’s design. As the party investigates deeper into the Desolate Chain, they slowly uncover the horrifying truth: the monsters are manufactured, the government is controlled, and the Trials themselves are nothing more than a cycle of suffering built to feed one man’s god complex.

## Key Roles
### Thorne Quell
Far above the skies of the Desolate Chain, beyond the reach of most people, Thorne Quell watches his world unfold from the deck of his colossal airborne vessel. The ship itself has become legendary. Built like an ancient wooden warship yet fused with modern arcane engineering, the massive flying construct drifts endlessly beyond the island, sustained by enormous glowing arcane cores embedded deep within its hull. Brass machinery groans beneath polished timber decks while magical energy pulses through veins carved into the ship’s structure.  From this airborne sanctuary, Thorne governs the Desolate Chain without ever truly touching it. He rarely appears in person. He does not need to. His influence reaches every corner of the islands through Quell Industries, through surveillance, through fear, and most importantly, through the voice that fills the arena.

Thorne supplies the High Regent with everything needed to sustain society on the islands: resources, technology, medicine, labor, and most importantly, participants for the Trials. Adventurers continue arriving because the system promises glory, wealth, purpose, and the possibility of defeating Krallax. Few realize they are simply feeding the cycle. Every decision is carefully calculated to keep the population exhausted, fearful, and dependent. Yet beneath all of this lies his true motivation.

Thorne does not merely want control — he wants worship. In a world abandoned by its gods, he wishes to replace them. He deliberately rules from the sky because he wants to be seen as something distant, untouchable, and divine. Every booming declaration in the arena, every life-or-death judgment, every desperate prayer whispered by contestants feeds his illusion of godhood. The Trials are not simply governance to him. They are ritual.

### High Regent Cealith Veyron
To the citizens of the Desolate Chain, [[High Regent Caelith Veyron]] is the iron ruler who keeps civilization alive amidst chaos. She oversees the Trials, commands the cities, and ensures order is maintained no matter the cost. Many fear her. Others admire her. Few truly understand her. The High Regent is not free.

Long ago, [[Thorne Quell]] altered her through a combination of mechanical augmentation and arcane manipulation. Beneath her composed exterior lies a body that no longer fully belongs to her. Her modifications allow Quell to monitor, influence, and if necessary directly control her actions. Disobedience is met with agony, loss of autonomy, or worse. And yet, over the years, she has convinced herself that obedience may be necessary. She has seen what the Desolate Chain becomes when order collapses. Violence spreads quickly. Entire districts descend into madness. Quell Industries keeps the islands functioning, and part of her fears that removing Thorne would doom everyone living there. That fear has slowly trapped her within the very system she hates.

At times, fragments of her old self still surface. She may secretly assist the party in subtle ways, offering hidden information or moments of mercy where she can. But every act of resistance carries enormous risk. The closer she moves toward rebellion, the tighter Quell’s control becomes. The people see a ruler. In reality, she is another prisoner.

## The Dollmaker
Deep beneath the Desolate Chain, hidden far from the public eye, the Dollmaker continues his work in isolation. To most citizens, he is little more than rumor — a name spoken in fearful whispers whenever strange creatures appear near the Trials. Some believe he creates monsters from corpses. Others claim he no longer resembles a man at all. The truth, as with most things tied to Quell Industries, is far more complicated. The Dollmaker is not the creator of the Whales.

Whales already exist long before they are ever brought to him. They are turning half-clankers — tragic individuals caught somewhere between humanity and mechanical corruption. Many are discovered by the [[Locust Knights]], wandering scouts and recruiters who search the world for those afflicted before their transformation becomes irreversible. The Knights travel alone across dangerous lands, guiding potential recruits back toward [[Figaro Spire]], the heavily fortified sanctuary that serves as the order’s headquarters.

The Dollmaker’s role begins after the Whales are captured. Under Thorne Quell’s control, he is forced to alter them, reshape them, and suppress what remains of their autonomy. Through painful arcane procedures and mechanical manipulation, he turns unstable beings into controllable weapons capable of being unleashed within the Trials. The process does not truly cure them. It merely redirects their violence. This is what makes his work so horrifying.

The contestants believe the Whales on this island are simply monsters bred for the arena, never realizing that many were once ordinary people who lost themselves to transformation. The Dollmaker preserves just enough of their minds to make them responsive to commands, while stripping away enough humanity to ensure they remain dangerous. In many cases, the Whales themselves suffer endlessly beneath this control.

Whether the Dollmaker performs this work willingly is difficult to determine. Years of forced obedience and isolation have twisted him emotionally. At times he appears disgusted by what he has become, speaking about the Whales with pity rather than cruelty. At other moments he treats his alterations like art, obsessively refining methods of restraint and aggression as though perfection might somehow justify the suffering involved. Thorne Quell understands the value of the Whales perfectly. They are living symbols of fear. Proof that corruption and transformation await anyone who loses control. By unleashing controlled Whales into the Trials, Quell reinforces the idea that survival belongs only to the strong while simultaneously hiding the truth of what these creatures really are. The Dollmaker knows this better than anyone. And somewhere beneath layers of guilt, exhaustion, and madness, he likely understands that every Whale he “fixes” is simply another victim trapped inside Quell’s machine.

## Companions (Mop)
Unlike the powerful figures controlling the Trials, Mop represents the ordinary people trapped inside Quell Industries’ machinery. Mop is not loyal because they believe in the system. They remain because Thorne Quell has made leaving impossible. Whether through debt, blackmail, threats, dependency, or some hidden leverage, Quell maintains a grip on them strong enough to ensure obedience. Like many others working beneath the corporation, Mop survives by compromising pieces of themselves day after day. This makes them dangerous in a different way than the others.

Mop sees the cracks within the system. They hear conversations not meant for outsiders. They witness fear among Quell’s employees, hidden shipments moving through restricted sectors, and moments where the illusion nearly falls apart. Unlike the [[High Regent Caelith Veyron]] or [[Characters/NPC/Backstory NPCs/The Dollmaker|The Dollmaker]], Mop still exists close enough to ordinary life to understand what the system is doing to people. As the party uncovers more of the truth, Mop becomes increasingly torn between survival and conscience. Fear may force them into betrayal. Guilt may push them toward rebellion. They may become one of the party’s greatest allies or another tragic victim consumed by Quell’s control. Because in the end, Quell Industries does not survive through monsters or machines alone. It survives because it traps people into believing they have no other choice.

There are many Companions working for Quell Industries. All representing different elements, hence they are elemental creatures. They are all assigned to a party until the next group of adventurers comes inn. When the party is killed they must remain in their domain until a new group is assigned to them. They are stuck by a elemental barrier put in place around the island, so they cannot exit their dimension outside of the Desolate Chain, they however can also not travel through this barrier.

# Plot line ending(s)
## Ending 1 — The Fall of Thorne Quell

After years of manipulation, fear, and bloodshed, the downfall of Thorne Quell does not come through war, but through deception.

High Regent Cealith Veyron, exhausted by decades of servitude beneath Quell Industries, finally reaches her breaking point. Whether through the party’s influence, the weakening of Quell’s control over her, or simply the realization that the Desolate Chain will never know peace while he lives, she secretly conspires against the man she once obeyed. A formal summons is sent to Thorne Quell. For perhaps the first time in years, the self-proclaimed god descends from the skies to personally visit the islands he rules from above. Confident in his power and blinded by his belief that nobody would dare rise against him, he arrives aboard his immense arcane airship expecting loyalty and obedience.

Instead, he walks into an ambush. The confrontation can take many forms depending on the party’s choices. Perhaps the battle erupts within the Regent’s palace while Quell’s voice desperately attempts to maintain control. Perhaps it takes place aboard the airship itself as arcane engines roar overhead. Or perhaps Cealith herself strikes the first blow against the man who imprisoned her for so long. When Thorne Quell finally dies, the illusion dies with him.

The booming divine voice vanishes from the arena forever. The Trials come to an end. The systems controlling the cities begin to collapse, but for the first time in generations, the people of the Desolate Chain are free to decide their own future rather than live beneath manufactured fear. The aftermath is uncertain, imperfect, and fragile. Some cities celebrate. Others descend briefly into chaos without Quell’s iron structure holding them together. Yet despite the instability, something long absent finally returns to the islands: Hope.

Cealith Veyron may attempt to rebuild the Chain honestly, no longer as a puppet ruler but as herself. The surviving Whales may finally be freed from forced control. The Dollmaker may disappear into obscurity, unable to live with what he has done. And the party becomes remembered not as Trial champions, but as the people who killed the false god above the clouds.
For the first time in living memory, the skies above the Desolate Chain fall silent.

# Ending 2 — The Dragon Beyond Krallax

The party reaches the end of the Trials after surviving horrors that countless adventurers before them could not. Against impossible odds, they finally stand before Krallax — the legendary beast the entire Desolate Chain was built around. For generations, this moment was believed to be impossible. The cities watch through projection magic and arcane broadcasts as the so-called Heroes of Isteria face the monster that defined their world. Thorne Quell himself speaks throughout the battle, his voice echoing across the arena like a god narrating the final chapter of a sacred tale. The people wait breathlessly for either triumph or tragedy. And somehow… The party begins to win. Krallax, the eternal nightmare, starts to fall beneath their combined strength. The illusion sustaining the Trials begins cracking in real time as the impossible becomes reality. Panic spreads quietly through Quell Industries. If Krallax dies, the system loses its purpose. The myth ends.

Then the sky opens.

Without warning, a deafening roar silences the arena. A massive shadow descends from above the clouds as a true dragon tears through the storm surrounding the Desolate Chain. Unlike Krallax, this creature is no engineered horror, no controlled monster maintained by Quell Industries. It is ancient, intelligent, and terrifyingly real — something closer to the forgotten gods of old than any beast the party has ever faced. The arrival changes everything instantly. Krallax, once believed to be the ultimate challenge, suddenly appears small in comparison. Whether the dragon was drawn by the battle, awakened by Krallax’s nearing death, or simply curious about the mortals calling themselves heroes is left uncertain. But one thing becomes immediately clear: The Trials were never a true test.

This is.

For the first time in his life, even Thorne Quell loses control of the narrative. His booming voice falters. The carefully constructed spectacle collapses into genuine panic as the dragon attacks indiscriminately, reducing sections of the arena and surrounding landscape to ruin. Contestants, soldiers, spectators, and Quell operatives alike are slaughtered as chaos erupts across the island. And in the middle of it all stand the party. The people of the Desolate Chain watch as the so-called Heroes of Isteria are faced with something beyond the manufactured horrors of the arena — a creature that cannot be controlled, manipulated, or turned into spectacle. A real legend. A real nightmare. Their final battle is no longer about winning the Trials. It becomes about proving whether heroes truly exist at all.

If the party defeats the dragon, their victory transcends everything Quell Industries ever created. Krallax may have been the symbol of the Trials, but the dragon becomes the moment that defines history itself. The people no longer see the party as contestants or champions. They become myths.

In the aftermath, the entire foundation of the Trials is shaken. The illusion of Thorne Quell’s godhood weakens, exposed by the arrival of a creature far older and greater than the false divinity he tried to imitate. Whether the Trials continue afterward becomes uncertain, but one truth can no longer be denied: The Heroes of Isteria faced a real challenge…and survived.
