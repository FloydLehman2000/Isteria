# Main Idea

Mudstitch Big Shroom’s story begins as one of expectation and rejection. Born as the bastard son of Droven Hillwarden, Mudstitch was never truly accepted within the noble structures of Gold Barrow despite carrying the blood of Hamfast the Golden himself. His green skin, inherited from his goblin mother, marked him immediately as different among the polished halls and golden farmlands of the Hillwarden estates. Though he carried the luck and wit of a halfling, there was always something sharper behind his eyes — the instinctive danger sense inherited from his mother’s bloodline. The name “Big Shroom” was never meant to honor him. It was a reminder. A bastard name.

Despite this, Mudstitch was still raised alongside the traditions of the Hillwarden family. He learned the politics of agriculture, the importance of the pipe weed trade, and the responsibilities carried by descendants of Hamfast the Golden. The Hillwardens were not merely landowners — they were guardians of prosperity itself. Their lands fed entire regions of Gold Barrow while the famous Toby Company spread Hillwarden influence through nearly every tavern, port, and trade road in the realm. Droven Hillwarden believed Mudstitch could still become useful. He was sent to the [[Grand Arcane Academy of Eldoria]] with a very clear purpose: study magical agriculture, return home, and increase the productivity of the old conquered lands through arcane means. It was a practical future. A controlled future. Mudstitch hated it.

Instead of applying for the Faculty of General Magical Knowledge, he joined the [[School of War]]. Not out of patriotism or duty, but because he longed for adventure beyond the suffocating expectations of his family. He wanted scars, stories, and purpose earned through struggle rather than inheritance. That choice led him toward the organization known as [[The Crimson Archive]]. To join the Archive, students of the School of War are required to complete a legendary undertaking worthy of remembrance. For many, this means slaying monsters or retrieving ancient relics. Mudstitch decided to do both.

After discovering a poster advertising Quell’s Trials, he realized the Desolate Chain offered exactly what he needed: danger, reputation, and the possibility of retrieving a long-lost Hillwarden family heirloom tied deeply to his bloodline. The shield lost by his grandfather decades ago. A relic once carried by generations of Hillwarden guardians before vanishing during a failed expedition long ago. Stories claim the shield only reveals its true magical abilities to those brave enough to stand firm against impossible odds. To Mudstitch, reclaiming the shield means more than completing a task for the Crimson Archive. It means reclaiming legitimacy. If he can survive the Trials, retrieve the heirloom, and return victorious, then perhaps history will remember him as a Hillwarden instead of merely a bastard.

Along the way, Mudstitch encounters Pyxis, who proposes they tackle the Quell Trials together. Whether through shared ambition, mutual survival, or genuine friendship, the two become bound together by the chaos awaiting them in the Desolate Chain. Yet beneath all of Mudstitch’s humor, smoking habits, and adventurous spirit lies a deeper fear: That no matter what he accomplishes… he may never truly belong anywhere.

# Key Roles
## Droven Hillwarden’s Role

Droven Hillwarden represents the crushing weight of legacy. As a descendant of Hamfast the Golden, Droven was raised to believe the Hillwarden bloodline existed to maintain stability, prosperity, and control over the lands won by their ancestors. Everything within Gold Barrow revolves around careful balance — trade, agriculture, politics, and family reputation. Mudstitch’s existence disrupted that balance.

Though Droven genuinely loved Mudstitch’s mother, their relationship became scandalous after the death of his wife. The birth of a green-skinned child outside noble marriage threatened the carefully maintained image of the Hillwarden family. Publicly, Droven treated Mudstitch as a responsibility rather than a son. Privately, things are more complicated. Droven sees potential in Mudstitch, but he fears what the world will do to him. Sending him to the [[Grand Arcane Academy of Eldoria]] was partly an attempt to secure a respectable future and partly an effort to distance him from Gold Barrow’s political scrutiny. When Mudstitch chose the School of War instead, Droven saw it as recklessness. But perhaps also inevitability. Because beneath all the family expectations, Mudstitch inherited the same adventurous spirit that once caused his grandfather to lose the Hillwarden shield in the first place.

# The Hillwarden Shield

The Hillwarden Shield was never truly lost. At least, not in the way Mudstitch’s family believes. Decades ago, Mudstitch’s grandfather ventured into the Desolate Chain alongside an expedition seeking glory, relics, and fortune within the Trials. During that journey, the expedition became entangled with the growing influence of Quell Industries and the emerging rule of [[High Regent Caelith Veyron]]. The details of what happened were buried intentionally.

What survived were rumors:
- betrayal within the expedition
- an impossible battle near the palace district
- a final stand made by Mudstitch’s grandfather against overwhelming odds

The shield disappeared shortly afterward. Unknown to the Hillwardens, the relic was claimed by Cealith Veyron herself and eventually placed within the throne room of her palace as a ceremonial trophy — a reminder that even noble bloodlines from beyond the Desolate Chain eventually break beneath its weight. To most citizens of the Chain, the shield is simply another ancient artifact decorating the palace. But those familiar with Gold Barrow immediately recognize it. 

The shield hangs directly behind the throne of the High Regent, suspended upon black iron mounts beneath the banners of Quell Industries and the crest of the Desolate Chain. Though polished and maintained, faint damage still scars its surface from the battle in which it was taken. This changes everything for Mudstitch. The relic is no longer hidden in forgotten ruins or buried deep underground. It has been displayed openly for years. The greatest shame of the Hillwarden family hangs in the heart of political power itself.

The Hillwarden Shield is large even by guardian standards, clearly forged not for elegance but endurance. Its shape resembles an elongated tower shield, broad enough to shelter nearly the entire body of the wielder. Time has worn its surface, yet the shield still carries an unmistakable presence — the kind that makes people instinctively lower their voices when standing before it. The metal itself is unusual.

Rather than polished steel, the shield appears forged from layered dark iron veined with faint gold running beneath the surface like cracks of sunlight trapped inside stone. The edges are reinforced with thick bronze bordering scarred by deep gouges and old battle damage that were never fully repaired. Whoever carried this shield was not concerned with appearance. Every dent upon it feels earned. At its center rests the crest of the Hillwarden bloodline. A series of rolling hills are engraved across the lower half of the shield, rising in layered curves like the farmlands of Gold Barrow itself. Towering above them stands a massive upright sword driven deep into the earth, its blade stretching almost the entire height of the shield. The sword does not resemble a weapon raised for conquest.

## High Regent Cealith Veyron’s Role

Long before she became the ruler of the Desolate Chain, Cealith Veyron was an adventurer. Few people still alive know this truth. In her younger years, before Quell Industries tightened its grip over the islands, Cealith traveled the Chain alongside a small expeditionary group seeking relics hidden beneath the chaos of the Trials. Among those adventurers was Mudstitch’s grandfather — the last known wielder of the Hillwarden Shield. The two once trusted each other deeply.

Stories describe Mudstitch’s grandfather as stubborn but fiercely protective, often placing himself between danger and the rest of the expedition. Cealith, meanwhile, was ambitious, intelligent, and already beginning to draw the attention of Thorne Quell long before she understood the cost of it. Their expedition eventually uncovered something of great interest to Quell Industries. What exactly they found has long since been buried beneath secrecy, but it was enough for Thorne Quell to intervene personally. Seeing opportunity in Cealith, he offered her survival, power, and purpose within the growing structure of the Desolate Chain.

In return, he demanded loyalty. When the expedition fell apart, Cealith betrayed the group on Quell’s orders. Mudstitch’s grandfather made a final stand to allow others to escape, wielding the Hillwarden Shield against overwhelming odds while Cealith abandoned him to his fate. Whether she believed he would survive, whether she regretted the decision immediately, or whether Quell forced her hand entirely is something only she truly knows. But she kept the shield.

Years later, after rising to become High Regent, she mounted it within her throne room as both a trophy and a reminder. To outsiders it appears as another relic decorating the palace halls. To Cealith, however, it became something far heavier — a symbol of the moment she surrendered the person she once was. Every time she sits upon the throne, the shield stands behind her silently watching. A guardian she betrayed. And perhaps the last piece of herself she never truly escaped.
